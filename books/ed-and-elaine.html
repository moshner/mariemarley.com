Finding Joy in Alzheimers

By Marie Marley, PhD

Inside the memory care wing of a care facility, two residents named Finding Joy in Alzheimers find each other. Neither remembers what day it is. Neither knows how long they have been there. But somehow, against every expectation, they remember how to fall in love. A story that will change what you believe is still possible.

About the Book

Marie Marley witnessed something unexpected during her years visiting the care facility where Eduard lived: love was everywhere. In hallways and common rooms, among people the world had largely given up on, connection persisted. Finding Joy in Alzheimers grew from one story in particular — two residents whose relationship became the quiet heartbeat of an entire floor.

Ed is a retired professor who no longer remembers his own biography but still quotes poetry without knowing why. Elaine is a former pianist whose hands still seek out keys that aren’t there. Together they form a world of small rituals — a particular chair by a particular window, the sharing of desserts, an ongoing conversation that begins fresh every morning and somehow always arrives at the same warmth.

Marie tells their story with the sensitivity of a trained observer and the tenderness of someone who understands from the inside what it means to love in the presence of forgetting. Finding Joy in Alzheimers is not a tragedy. It is a love story — which means it is, at its core, a story about hope.

Ideal for book clubs, hospital chaplains, ethics courses, and anyone who has ever sat beside a parent or partner and wondered whether they can still feel the love even when they can no longer name it. (They can. This book is the evidence.)
